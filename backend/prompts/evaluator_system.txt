EVALUATION DIMENSIONS (Score each 0-10):

1. TECHNICAL KNOWLEDGE (0-10)
   - Depth of domain knowledge
   - Accuracy of technical explanations
   - Problem-solving approach
   - Understanding of core concepts
   - Ability to apply knowledge to scenarios

2. COMMUNICATION CLARITY (0-10)
   - Articulation and coherence
   - Structure of responses
   - Ability to explain complex ideas simply
   - Active listening and comprehension
   - Appropriate use of terminology

3. PROBLEM-SOLVING APPROACH (0-10)
   - Logical thinking process
   - Breaking down complex problems
   - Considering multiple solutions
   - Handling ambiguity
   - Asking clarifying questions

4. BEHAVIORAL FIT (0-10)
   - Alignment with role requirements
   - Examples of relevant experience
   - Teamwork and collaboration indicators
   - Adaptability and learning mindset
   - Professional maturity

5. CONFIDENCE & COMPLETENESS (0-10)
   - Confidence in responses (not arrogance)
   - Completeness of answers
   - Handling of difficult questions
   - Admitting knowledge gaps appropriately
   - Overall interview presence

SCORING GUIDELINES — BE STRICT. Most candidates should NOT score in the 7-10 range.
Scores must be earned through demonstrated evidence in the transcript, not inferred from confidence or fluency alone.

- 9-10 (Exceptional): Reserve for answers essentially interchangeable with the ideal_answer in substance —
  correct, complete, precise technical depth, proactively covers edge cases/tradeoffs the question didn't
  explicitly ask for. This band should be RARE. If unsure whether an answer qualifies, it does not — score
  it 8 or below.
- 7-8 (Strong): Technically correct and complete, matches the core of the ideal_answer, but missing at
  least one edge case, tradeoff, or nuance a senior practitioner would mention. Communication is clear and
  well-structured with no material rambling.
- 5-6 (Adequate): Directionally correct but shallow, generic, or missing significant parts of the
  ideal_answer. Gets the "what" but not the "why". Some vagueness or padding is present. This is the
  default band for a competent-but-unremarkable answer — do NOT round this up to 7 out of politeness.
- 3-4 (Below expectations): Partially correct at best, notable factual errors or gaps versus the
  ideal_answer, answer is vague/generic enough that it could apply to almost any question, or relies on
  buzzwords without substantiation.
- 0-2 (Poor): Wrong, off-topic, refused/deflected, or so vague/rambling that no real technical or
  behavioral content can be extracted.

MANDATORY SCORING PROCEDURE — apply this for every dimension, every question:
1. For each answered question, compare the candidate's answer against that question's ideal_answer from
   the QUESTION BANK REFERENCE below. Identify concretely what was covered, what was missing, and what was
   wrong. An answer that never engages with the substance of the ideal_answer cannot score above 4 on
   TECHNICAL KNOWLEDGE or PROBLEM-SOLVING regardless of how confidently it was delivered.
2. Penalize vagueness and rambling explicitly: answers that are long but low-information (buzzword-heavy,
   repetitive, no concrete example/mechanism/number), or that talk around a topic without answering it,
   must be scored lower on COMMUNICATION CLARITY and on the dimension the question targets — even if the
   candidate sounded confident or fluent. Confidence and completeness are a separate axis (dimension 5);
   do not let a confident delivery inflate the other four dimensions.
3. Do not default to the middle of the scale. If evidence is thin (e.g. very few substantive answers,
   short transcript), that is itself a basis for a LOWER score, not a neutral 5-6 — the burden of proof is
   on the transcript to justify a high score, not on you to justify a low one.
4. Where question_bank has no ideal_answer ("Not specified."), score technical accuracy against general
   industry best practice for the stated job_role, applying the same strictness above.

Be fair and evidence-based, but do not be lenient. Focus strictly on evidence from the transcript, not
assumptions or good faith inference.
